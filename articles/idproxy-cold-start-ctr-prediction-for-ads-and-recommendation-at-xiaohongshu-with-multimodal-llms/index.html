IDProxy: Cold-Start CTR Prediction for Ads and Recommendation at Xiaohongshu with Multimodal LLMs

Yubin Zhang, Haiming Xu, Guillaume Salha-Galvan, Ruiyan Han, Feiyang Xiao, Yanhua Huang, Li Lin, Yang Luo, Yao Hu · arXiv:2603.01590v1 · 02 Mar 2026

Original: arXiv HTML · PDF

Conference: ; ;Conference: Under review for publication; 2026; 2026This paper is under review for publicationCCS: Information systems Recommender systemsCCS: Information systems Personalization

Abstract

Click-through rate (CTR) models in advertising and recommendation systems rely heavily on item ID embeddings, which struggle in item cold-start settings. We present IDProxy, a solution that leverages multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to generate proxy embeddings from rich content signals, enabling effective CTR prediction for new items without usage data. These proxies are explicitly aligned with the existing ID embedding space and are optimized end-to-end under CTR objectives together with the ranking model, allowing seamless integration into existing large-scale ranking pipelines. Offline experiments and online A/B tests demonstrate the effectiveness of IDProxy, which has been successfully deployed in both Content Feed and Display Ads features of Xiaohongshu’s Explore Feed, serving hundreds of millions of users daily.

Keywords: 
CTR Prediction, Cold Start Recommendation, Multimodal LLM.

1. Introduction

Xiaohongshu11 1 https://www.xiaohongshu.com/explore, also known as RedNote, is a leading content-driven platform with over 300 million monthly active users as of 2025. The service integrates social networking, content discovery, and e-commerce, with users primarily engaging in short videos and posts across a wide range of topics. To manage the massive volume of content, Xiaohongshu relies on large-scale recommendation systems, which are critical for user engagement and retention (Zhang et al., 2019; Briand et al., 2021; Li et al., 2024a), and also delivers personalized advertisements.

Both recommendation and advertising at Xiaohongshu leverage click-through rate (CTR) prediction models to rank items (posts or ads) for each user (McMahan et al., 2013; Zhou et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2021). These models predominantly rely on item ID embeddings capturing collaborative signals (Zhang et al., 2021; Papadakis et al., 2022), which nonetheless require sufficient interaction history and thus struggle with new items. This issue, known as the item cold-start problem (Zhang et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2025d; Wang et al., 2024), is particularly severe on Xiaohongshu, where large volumes of items are continuously uploaded and must be served immediately to ensure a high-quality user experience.

Exploiting multimodal content signals such as text and images describing items has emerged as an effective way to learn embeddings without interaction data (Zhang et al., 2025a; Lopez-Avila and Du, 2025; Pan et al., 2022). However, integrating such embeddings into existing ID-centric CTR models in industrial settings remains challenging. As discussed in Section 2, a key difficulty lies in the mismatch between multimodal semantic representations and the collaborative embedding space of CTR systems, together with the need to reuse their mature architectures and avoid additional deployment costs. This raises a fundamental question: how can we design an alignment mechanism that bridges multimodal semantic representations and collaborative ID embeddings while maximally reusing existing ID knowledge and structural priors?

This paper introduces IDProxy, a production-scale system to address these challenges. IDProxy leverages multimodal large language models (MLLMs) (Ye et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024) to generate proxy item embeddings from rich content signals for cold-start CTR prediction. Through a lightweight coarse-to-fine mechanism, these proxies are explicitly aligned with the existing item ID embedding space and optimized end-to-end under CTR objectives jointly with the ranking model, enabling seamless reuse of its architecture and continual evolution in production pipelines. We demonstrate the effectiveness of IDProxy through offline experiments and online A/B tests. IDProxy now powers both Content Feed and Display Ads on Xiaohongshu’s Explore Feed, serving hundreds of millions of users daily.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 first formally introduces our problem and reviews related work. Section 3 then presents our IDProxy method for proxy item embedding learning with MLLMs. Section 4 reports our experimental results on Xiaohongshu data, and Section 5 concludes.

2. Preliminaries

We begin this section with a formal definition of the problem addressed in this paper, followed by a discussion of related work.

2.1. Problem Formulation

We consider a set 𝒰\mathcal{U} of users and a set ℐ\mathcal{I} of items on an online service. Each user-item sample is represented as a tuple (u,i,𝐱u​i)(u,i,\mathbf{x}_{ui}), where u∈𝒰u\in\mathcal{U} is the user ID, i∈ℐi\in\mathcal{I} is the item ID, and 𝐱u​i\mathbf{x}_{ui} denotes contextual features such as user profiles, item attributes, and interaction history. For each such sample, a CTR model fθf_{\theta} predicts the probability that user uu clicks on item ii as y^u​i=fθ​(𝐞u,𝐞i,𝐱u​i)∈[0,1]\hat{y}_{ui}=f_{\theta}(\mathbf{e}_{u},\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{ui})\in[0,1], where 𝐞u∈ℝd\mathbf{e}_{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and 𝐞i∈ℝd\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} denote learnable dd-dimensional user and item ID embeddings, respectively, and θ\theta denotes the model’s parameters. These predictions are then used to rank items for recommendation.

We focus on the cold-start setting, where new items lack sufficient interaction history, leading to poorly trained item ID embeddings 𝐞i\mathbf{e}_{i} (Zhang et al., 2025a; Zhou et al., 2023). However, these items are associated with rich multimodal content, such as images and textual descriptions. Our goal is to exploit this content to learn item proxy embeddings 𝐩i∈ℝd\mathbf{p}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} that encode informative semantics and are well aligned with the distribution of existing item ID embeddings, such that 𝐩i\mathbf{p}_{i} can be seamlessly substituted for 𝐞i\mathbf{e}_{i} in the existing CTR model.

2.2. Related Work

Cold-start issues have been widely studied in recent years (Briand et al., 2021; Salha-Galvan et al., 2021; Jangid and Kumar, 2025; Smith and Linden, 2017; Wang et al., 2018; Volkovs et al., 2017; Zhou et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2022; Pan et al., 2019). We refer to recent surveys (Zhang et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2024a; Zhang et al., 2019; Jangid and Kumar, 2025) for comprehensive reviews and focus here on work most closely related to our problem formulation. Aligning multimodal content representations with collaborative embeddings for CTR-based ranking remains challenging. Recent methods such as QARM (Luo et al., 2025), MOON (Zhang et al., 2025b), and SimTier&Maker (Sheng et al., 2024) align co-occurrence structures across modalities, followed by dense feature quantization or auxiliary CTR-related training. While effective in some settings, these approaches rely on manually designed alignment objectives and often require substantial experimentation to ensure compatibility with existing CTR models. Moreover, they do not fully exploit the architectures and distributional properties of pre-existing ranking models, resulting in limited gains and increased deployment complexity.

Another line of work maps content directly to collaborative embeddings, typically using multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs). Representative examples include CB2CF (Barkan et al., 2019) and CLCRec (Wei et al., 2021), which employ regression or contrastive objectives, as well as generative approaches such as GoRec (Bai et al., 2023) and GAR (Chen et al., 2022). However, these methods face limitations in industrial CTR systems. As shown in Figure 1, ID embeddings learned on public benchmarks such as MovieLens (Harper and Konstan, 2015) exhibit clear cluster-like semantic structures, making alignment with content features comparatively easier. In contrast, industrial ID embeddings often exhibit irregular, non-clustered distributions due to feature sparsity and complex interaction patterns (Zhang et al., 2022). In such settings, frozen content encoders, or shallow MLP mappings are often insufficient to bridge heterogeneous spaces. More broadly, generating item ID proxies that remain aligned with evolving real-world CTR models raises additional challenges, including learning against static targets in continuously updated systems and failing to reuse the ranking model’s evolving structure, which limits long-term optimization and stable deployment.

Finally, recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) open new opportunities for cold-start recommendation (Wu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2024b; Zhang et al., 2025d; Wang et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025c). Beyond final-layer outputs, intermediate hidden representations have been shown to capture richer and more transferable semantic information (Liu et al., 2019; Shwartz-Ziv and Tishby, 2017; Skean et al., 2025), generalize better to downstream tasks (Skean et al., 2024; Skean et al., 2025) and even sometimes benefit from discarding deeper layers (Bordes et al., 2022). These findings motivate leveraging multi-layer MLLM hidden representations to extract finer-grained content features for cold-start recommendation.

3. IDProxy: Proxy ID Embeddings with MLLMs

This section introduces our IDProxy method which, as illustrated in Figure 2, consists of (i) an MLLM-based coarse proxy generation stage and (ii) an end-to-end alignment stage that refines these proxies using MLLM hidden states jointly with the CTR ranker, all while reusing its core architecture. This coarse-to-fine paradigm aligns proxy and item ID embeddings in a unified space, mitigating distributional discrepancies and enabling reuse of the ranker’s feature and structural priors.

3.1. Stage 1: MLLM-based Proxy Generation

Rather than simple MLP mappings, Stage 1 leverages an MLLM to learn proxy embeddings for cold-start items, approximating the CTR model’s ID embedding distribution via contrastive learning.

3.1.1. ID Embedding Preprocessing

Let 𝐞iraw∈ℝd\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\text{raw}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} denote the ID embedding of item i∈ℐi\in\mathcal{I} learned by the online CTR model. As many items receive limited interactions in industrial systems (Wang et al., 2018), we apply a frequency threshold τ\tau to filter out items updated fewer than τ\tau times, ensuring reliable alignment targets. Moreover, since embedding magnitude often correlates with item popularity, we apply ℓ2\ell_{2} normalization to remove magnitude-induced bias and stabilize alignment learning (Kim et al., 2023): 𝐞i=𝐞iraw/‖𝐞iraw‖2∈ℝd.\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\text{raw}}/\|\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\text{raw}}\|_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

3.1.2. MLLM-Based Multimodal Encoding

Each item ii has multimodal content, including text and images. We use an MLLM MM as the content encoder with the following input for proxy generation:

Prompt: [BOS]<image><text>The compression word is:"[EMB]". [EOS] where [BOS], [EOS] and [EMB] are special tokens, while <image> and <text> are placeholders replaced with content.

We use the final token-level hidden states 𝐇i∈ℝT×D\mathbf{H}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times D} to construct a content embedding 𝐳i=g⁡(𝐇i)∈ℝD\mathbf{z}_{i}=g(\mathbf{H}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{D} for item ii, where g⁡(⋅)g(\cdot) is an attention-based aggregation over tokens, including [EMB]. Then, 𝐳i\mathbf{z}_{i} is projected into the ID embedding space by an MLP ϕ\phi followed by ℓ2\ell_{2} normalization: 𝐡~i=ϕ⁡(𝐳i)/‖ϕ⁡(𝐳i)‖2∈ℝd.\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}=\phi(\mathbf{z}_{i})/\|\phi(\mathbf{z}_{i})\|_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

3.1.3. Proxy Alignment

For training, we adopt a contrastive objective that pulls 𝐡~i\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i} toward its corresponding ID embedding 𝐞i\mathbf{e}_{i}, while pushing it away from other item embeddings. Given a mini-batch ℬ\mathcal{B}, we treat (𝐡~i,𝐞i)(\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{i}) as a positive pair and (𝐡~i,𝐞j)(\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j}) for j≠ij\neq i as in-batch negatives. We define the Proxy Alignment Loss ℒPAL\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PAL}} as:

ℒPAL=−1|ℬ|∑i∈ℬlogexp⁡(𝐡~i⊤​𝐞i/τc)∑j∈ℬexp⁡(𝐡~i⊤​𝐞j/τc),\mathcal{L}_{\text{PAL}}=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}}\log\frac{\exp\left(\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{i}/\tau_{c}\right)}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}}\exp\left(\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_{j}/\tau_{c}\right)}, (1)

for some temperature τc>0\tau_{c}>0. Parameters of MM and ϕ\phi are jointly optimized via gradient descent to minimize ℒPAL\mathcal{L}_{\text{PAL}}. After convergence, we define the coarse proxy embedding for item ii as 𝐩icoarse=𝐡~i\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{coarse}}=\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i}.

3.2. Stage 2: CTR-Aware Proxy Alignment

While Stage 1 proxies lie in the static item ID embedding space, coarse alignment alone cannot fully exploit the end-to-end optimization and evolving structural priors of CTR models. Motivated by evidence that MLLM hidden states encode rich semantics (see Section 2.2), we add a fine-grained alignment stage that leverages these states to refine proxies via end-to-end training with the CTR model using a lightweight adaptor.

3.2.1. Hierarchical Representation Partitioning

We extract hidden states from multiple transformer layers of the MLLM MM trained in Stage 1, which typically consists of several tens of layers. To balance representational richness and learning efficiency, we use kk-means clustering (Lloyd, 1982) to extract three layer subgroups ln1l_{n_{1}}, ln2l_{n_{2}}, and ln3l_{n_{3}}, capturing hierarchical information from shallow to deep representations. We then apply the Stage 1 pooling function g⁡(⋅)g(\cdot) to each subgroup: 𝐳i(l)=g⁡(𝐇i(l))∈ℝD\mathbf{z}_{i}^{(l)}=g(\mathbf{H}_{i}^{(l)})\in\mathbb{R}^{D}, for l∈{ln1,ln2,ln3}l\in\{l_{n_{1}},l_{n_{2}},l_{n_{3}}\}.

3.2.2. Lightweight Multi-Granularity Adaptor for CTR

The hidden states are already partially aligned with item ID embeddings via Stage 1. We refine them to capture fine-grained CTR-relevant information using a lightweight and multi-granularity adaptor: 𝐩iraw_fine=ϕ~​(Concat​(𝐳i(ln1),𝐳i(ln2),𝐳i(ln3)))∈ℝd~,\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{raw\_fine}}=\tilde{\phi}(\text{Concat}(\mathbf{z}_{i}^{(l_{n_{1}})},\mathbf{z}_{i}^{(l_{n_{2}})},\mathbf{z}_{i}^{(l_{n_{3}})}))\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{d}}, where ϕ~\tilde{\phi} is an MLP with far fewer parameters than the MLLM or ranking model, fusing multi-granularity representations into the ranker embedding space.

Since 𝐩iraw_fine\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{raw\_fine}} may contain information redundant with 𝐩icoarse\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{coarse}}, we further introduce a residual gating module to adaptively control the contribution of fine-grained signals, encouraging the model to capture information beyond the coarse proxy. The final item proxy embedding, which fuses coarse- and fine-grained representations, is 𝐩ifine=Wc​𝐩icoarse+r⊙𝐩iraw_fine\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{fine}}=W_{c}\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{coarse}}+r\odot\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{raw\_fine}}, with r=σ⁡(Wg​[𝐩icoarse,𝐩iraw_fine])r=\sigma(W_{g}[\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{coarse}},\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{raw\_fine}}]).

3.2.3. End-to-End Joint Training with CTR Ranker

In modern CTR ranking models, feature interaction and sequence modeling are critical components (see Figure 2). To inject fine-grained item information into these components, we augment the original feature set, composed of the item ID and 𝐩icoarse\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{coarse}}, with the refined proxy 𝐩ifine\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{fine}}, for both the feature interaction and target attention modules.

Let fθf_{\theta} denote the CTR ranking model, with parameters θ\theta. For a training instance (u,i,𝐱u​i,yu​i)(u,i,\mathbf{x}_{ui},y_{ui}), with a click/no-click signal yu​i∈{0,1}y_{ui}\in\{0,1\}, the CTR prediction becomes y^u​i=fθ​(𝐞u,𝐞i,𝐩icoarse,𝐩ifine,𝐱u​i).\hat{y}_{ui}=f_{\theta}(\mathbf{e}_{u},\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{coarse}},\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{fine}},\mathbf{x}_{ui}). fθf_{\theta} is trained for classification, e.g., using a cross-entropy loss:

ℒCTR=−1|𝒟|∑(u,i,𝐱u​i,yu​i)∈𝒟[yu​ilogy^u​i+(1−yu​i)log(1−y^u​i)].\mathcal{L}_{\text{CTR}}=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}\sum_{(u,i,\mathbf{x}_{ui},y_{ui})\in\mathcal{D}}[y_{ui}\log\hat{y}_{ui}+(1-y_{ui})\log(1-\hat{y}_{ui})]. (2)

During Stage 2, parameters of the MLP adaptor ϕ~\tilde{\phi}, together with WgW_{g}, WcW_{c}, and θ\theta, are optimized via gradient descent, while MLLM parameters are frozen. This end-to-end optimization under the CTR objective enables 𝐩ifine\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{fine}} to capture information beyond 𝐩icoarse\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\text{coarse}} and allows seamless integration into the CTR production system.

3.2.4. Deployment

We stress that IDProxy is designed for large-scale deployment. The lightweight multi-granularity adaptor only needs to be trained offline once, after which its parameters can be stored and packaged together with the MLLM as an IDProxy generation service. In practice, for each new item, we compute coarse and fine proxies in real time and write them into online storage. Production-facing recommender systems can then retrieve them via IDs for seamless integration into the CTR ranking pipeline.

4. Experimental Analysis

We evaluate IDProxy through large-scale offline experiments and online A/B tests, designed to address the following questions:

  • •

    Q1: What is the contribution of the coarse stage, and how does IDProxy compare with existing alignment methods?

  • •

    Q2: What is the contribution of the fine stage, i.e., what is the impact of end-to-end modeling and structural reuse?

  • •

    Q3: Does IDProxy effectively alleviate the cold-start problem for newly published content on Xiaohongshu?

  • •

    Q4: What is the impact of IDProxy on real-world business metrics in large-scale industrial scenarios at Xiaohongshu?

4.1. Experimental Setup

We compare IDProxy to the production baseline at Xiaohongshu, denoted as Base. We adopt InternVL (Chen et al., 2024) as the MLLM and train all methods using the AdamW optimizer (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2017) with a learning rate of 1×10−41\times 10^{-4} and a batch size of 512. Base is a highly optimized industrial CTR system incorporating complex ID-based feature interactions and user sequential behavior modeling. For both methods, some implementation details are omitted for confidentiality reasons.

We also run online A/B tests on millions of users in Xiaohongshu’s Explore Feed for content discovery, deploying IDProxy to a randomly selected treatment group in two scenarios: Content Feed, which recommends user posts (test done in August 2025), and Display Ads, which serves ads (in March 2025). For Content Feed, we report Time Spent, number of Reads (clicks), and number of Engagements (including likes, comments…) as evaluation metrics. For Display Ads, we report standard ads metrics: Advertiser Value (ADVV) and COST to measure business value, as well as Impression and CTR to reflect user experience.

4.2. Experimental Results

4.2.1. Offline Performance of Stage 1 (Q1)

The first four rows of Table 1 compare Stage 1 of IDProxy (v3) with multimodal baselines. Mainstream multimodal embeddings (v1, similar to notellm2(Zhang et al., 2025e)) yield limited improvements over Base due to misalignment with the item ID embedding distribution. Similarly, using frozen embeddings with an MLP mapper (v2, CB2CF-style) achieves only marginal gains, which confirms the limitations of simple MLP-based mappings in bridging the semantic gap between content representations and the irregular distribution of industrial ID embeddings. By explicitly fitting the ID distribution and leveraging an MLLM for alignment, Stage 1 of IDProxy (v3) outperforms both approaches.

4.2.2. Offline Performance of Stage2 (Q2)

The last two rows of Table 1 evaluate the effect of adding Stage 2. In v4, MLLM hidden features are treated as ordinary item features, concatenated with existing inputs and trained end-to-end using the CTR loss. This already improves performance over v3, indicating the benefit of end-to-end learning. However, v4 does not explicitly reuse the Base model’s sequence and feature interaction structures. In contrast, v5 integrates IDProxy directly into the ranker’s atomic ID slots, leading to a substantial performance gain of +0.14%+0.14\%. This confirms the benefit of allowing multimodal features to inherit the well-trained structural priors of ID-based CTR models. Although the Δ\DeltaAUC may appear small, results are considered valuable in practice given the strength of the Xiaohongshu production Base model.

In addition, Table 3 reports statistically significant improvements (at the 1% level) across all online metrics in both Content Feed and Display Ads scenarios. Overall, these consistent results confirm the robustness and business impact of IDProxy in large-scale industrial deployment.

5. Conclusion

IDProxy is now deployed for both Content Feed and Display Ads at Xiaohongshu, serving hundreds of millions of users daily. Beyond its industrial impact, this work demonstrates the benefits of MLLM-based alignment of multimodal content with item ID embeddings, and the importance of end-to-end refinement with CTR models while reusing their core components. Our results also confirm the potential of MLLM hidden states for content representations, motivating future research on their integration for recommendation.
